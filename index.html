Ripples and glottal flow

Three cycles of your voice, closures marked.

Your sound, filtered to keep fR1's ringing. Each closure sets fR1 ringing; count the ripples between closures.

Glottal flow: the same cycles with the resonances taken out. The flat stretches are the closed phase; the humps are the folds opening.

Each closure is a tap that sets the tract ringing at fR1, so the number of ripples per cycle is fR1 ÷ fo. The ripples fade as they go, and the next closure starts them again.

How the label is read

fR1, H1 and H2 are each rounded to their nearest piano key, the same rule the Vowel Synth Lab uses. Open: fR1 on H2's key or higher. Close: fR1 more than a half step above H1 but below H2. Whoop: fR1 on H1's key or a half step either side. Below that, H1 has passed above fR1.

When fR1 is within about a half step of one of those lines, the readout says so. Small changes in vowel or measurement can tip it either way.

More settings

The lab works out which way up the signal is from the closures. If the flow looks upside down (sharp rises instead of sharp falls), tick this.

What it can measure

Reliable from the bass staff up to about B4, for any voice, on a steady note held for a third of a second or more. Above about C5 the harmonics are too far apart to find fR1. Vibrato is fine. Fast runs and slides read only once the pitch settles.

About

Acoustic Registration Lab was made by Chadley Ballantyne (Stetson University) as the reverse of the Vowel Synth Lab: instead of setting fR1 and hearing the result, you sing and the lab measures fR1 in your own voice. It uses the same closed-phase analysis as the Inverse Filter Lab. It finds each glottal closure, fits a linear predictor only to the stretch of each cycle when the folds are closed and the tract rings on its own, and keeps the fit that leaves the flattest closed phase. The labels open, close and whoop follow Kenneth Bozeman's acoustic registration terms. Built with Claude.
